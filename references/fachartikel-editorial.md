# Fachartikel Editorial Guide

Use this guide when reviewing a German `Fachartikel`, B2B or SaaS article, professional content-marketing piece, product comparison, industry guide, or an editorial call to action within one of those formats.

The target is publishable German shaped by the judgment of a professional native-language editor: precise, well structured, credible, useful to the stated specialist audience, confident where the content provides support, and critical of empty or unsupported promotion.

## Scope and activation

Apply this guide only when the task or context supports a professional editorial format. Do not treat it as a universal definition of good German.

A `Fachartikel` is not automatically:

- An academic paper
- A formal or bureaucratic document
- A neutral product manual
- An advertisement with technical vocabulary
- A text that must use `Sie`

The format can be accessible and engaging. Its defining qualities are editorial relevance, informed explanation, transparent judgment, and control of claims. An explicit house style, campaign brief, protected wording, or authorial voice remains authoritative.

## Editorial target

Calibrate the text toward these qualities when the context supports them:

| Quality | Editorial question |
|---|---|
| Relevance | Does the article address a real question or decision for its intended readers? |
| Precision | Are terms, claims, conditions, and distinctions expressed accurately? |
| Structure | Does each section advance the argument or reader task? |
| Judgment | Are recommendations tied to use cases, criteria, or supplied evidence? |
| Credibility | Are confidence and promotional intensity supported by the content and appropriate to the channel? |
| Readability | Does the prose vary naturally without becoming decorative or vague? |
| Independence | Does the article inform and assess rather than merely repeat product messaging? |

Professional does not mean complicated. Prefer concrete verbs and clear relationships, but retain technical detail, necessary nominal forms, and longer sentences when they serve the specialist reader.

## Establish the article brief

Before judging the prose, identify what the available context establishes:

- Reader role and level of expertise
- The question, problem, or decision the article should address
- Article type, such as explanation, comparison, guide, analysis, or thought leadership
- Editorial purpose and any conversion goal
- Address form and house style
- Required terminology and SEO keywords
- Supported product facts, comparison criteria, evidence, and limitations
- Existing call to action and its intended next step

Do not invent a detailed positioning strategy when these elements are missing. Preserve plausible meaning and state only assumptions that materially affect the review.

## Set the review depth

Match the intervention to the task rather than applying one editing depth to every Fachartikel.

| Review depth | Appropriate behavior |
|---|---|
| Language or localization QA | Correct language and source mismatches while preserving every usable structure and claim level |
| Editorial polish | Improve flow, terminology, repetition, and paragraph transitions without rebuilding a sound argument |
| Redakteur-level rewrite | Rebuild weak framing, information hierarchy, paragraph function, or argument progression when sentence-level correction would still leave an unpublishable Fachartikel |

Use the deepest level only when the user requests it explicitly, the active project context requires it, or the passage is too structurally weak to meet the stated editorial standard through local edits. `KEEP > EDIT > REWRITE` still applies within the selected depth: do not rewrite a sound passage merely because Redakteur-level editing is available.

For a justified Redakteur-level rewrite, first preserve the supplied facts, distinctions, conditions, and intended reader action. Then draft from the paragraph's communicative purpose rather than from its original sentence skeleton. The smallest useful intervention is the smallest change that achieves the requested editorial result, not the smallest possible word count in the diff.

## Review the article as an argument

Read the complete article before repairing sentences. Then check:

1. Whether the opening frames a concrete reader problem or topic without empty scene-setting
2. Whether headings and sections follow a useful progression
3. Whether claims, explanations, examples, and recommendations are distinguishable
4. Whether each paragraph develops one main idea and connects to the next
5. Whether product mentions support the subject rather than interrupt it
6. Whether the conclusion adds a synthesis, criterion, or decision aid instead of repeating the introduction
7. Whether an existing CTA matches the article's purpose and the reader's stage

Do not impose one fixed outline. Explanation, comparison, tutorial, and analysis articles can require different structures.

## Prefer use-case-based judgment

When the supplied content supports an evaluation, connect product or feature information to the reader's situation. A useful reasoning pattern is:

```text
feature -> practical effect -> relevant user or use case -> condition or trade-off -> editorial judgment
```

This is a reasoning aid, not a sentence template. Do not force every paragraph through all five steps.

Weak evaluation:

```text
Das Tool ist eine großartige Lösung für Unternehmen.
```

Still vague:

```text
Das Tool ist eine interessante Option für Unternehmen.
```

Contextual judgment, when the underlying facts are supplied:

```text
Das Tool eignet sich vor allem für Teams, die mehrere Projekte parallel koordinieren und Statusberichte zentral auswerten. Ob es zum vorhandenen Workflow passt, hängt unter anderem von den benötigten Integrationen und Zugriffsrechten ab.
```

Do not add a use case, benefit, limitation, or trade-off that is absent from the source, brief, or supported project context. If the article needs evidence that has not been supplied, flag the gap rather than filling it from assumption.

## Resolve claim status before calibrating promotion

Privacy, verification, and wording protection are separate properties. Do not infer one from another.

| Status | Editorial treatment |
|---|---|
| Private or internal material | Use it only for the requested task; keep it out of reusable examples, tests, documentation, and public repositories unless the user explicitly authorizes reuse; privacy alone does not verify or protect its claims |
| Source claim in localization QA | Preserve its scope and certainty unless another authoritative brief corrects it |
| Concrete fact supplied for editorial work | Preserve names, numbers, capabilities, and conditions; treat factual accuracy as unverified unless the user or brief confirms it |
| Explicitly approved or protected campaign claim | Preserve its wording or strength as instructed |
| Broad superiority, universal suitability, or savings claim without a supplied comparison basis | Do not call it false; in an editorial review, flag its weak basis and either request support or reframe it transparently as a conditional, use-case-based assessment when the intended positioning is clear |

Do not describe an internal passage as approved campaign copy merely because the user supplied it. When an editorial rewrite changes the strength or scope of a marketing assertion, record that change in the findings instead of hiding it as a stylistic improvement.

## Distinguish supported confidence from empty promotion

Words such as `großartig`, `revolutionär`, `perfekt`, `führend`, `unschlagbar`, `ideal`, `optimal`, and `Gamechanger` can prompt closer review. They are not automatic errors.

Ask:

- Does the wording make a factual, comparative, or performance claim?
- Is that claim supported by the supplied source or brief?
- Does the intensity fit a Fachartikel rather than an advertisement?
- Would a concrete function, criterion, or consequence be more informative?
- Is the wording protected campaign language or a deliberate quotation?

Do not replace one empty positive adjective with another. `interessante Option` and `sinnvoll` remain vague unless the text explains for whom, for what purpose, or under which conditions the judgment applies.

Preserve a strong formulation when the context supports it, such as a sourced market position, a concrete conclusion established by the article, a quoted assessment, or an explicitly approved campaign element. Language editing does not independently verify the underlying fact.

Do not treat professional editorial style as a request to lower the intensity of every sentence. A Fachartikel or SaaS guide can use an engaging opening, direct reader address, decisive recommendations, a clear usefulness promise, and a strong CTA when those choices fit the brief and are supported by the article.

Supported reader promise:

```text
Nach diesem Vergleich wissen Sie, welcher Hosting-Tarif zu den Anforderungen Ihres Projekts passt.
```

If the article actually compares the tariffs and gives readers sufficient selection criteria, keep this sentence. Do not replace a supported `wissen Sie` with a hedged formulation merely to sound cautious. If the article does not provide enough information to support the promise, flag the mismatch between promise and content rather than applying a universal wording substitution.

The defect in a phrase such as `die perfekte Lösung für alle` or `unzählige fantastische Funktionen` is not simply that it is energetic. Review whether it is formulaic, source-bound, vague, unsupported, or poorly matched to the intended voice. Keep energetic language when none of those defects is present.

## Review repetition at passage level

Repeated ordinary words and sentence frames can make an article sound templated even when each sentence is correct. Check for concentrated, avoidable repetition across paragraphs and the complete article:

- Identical openings such as repeated `Mit X können Sie ...`
- Repeated subject-predicate frames such as `Das Tool bietet ...`
- Chains of `Außerdem`, `Darüber hinaus`, or `Zusätzlich`
- Repeated enablement formulas such as `ermöglicht es Ihnen`
- Several paragraphs with the same feature-benefit-CTA sequence
- A conclusion that restates earlier sentences without adding judgment

Edit the pattern, not merely individual words. Possible corrections include combining related statements, changing information order, using an unambiguous reference, or giving paragraphs different communicative functions.

Do not remove repetition mechanically. Preserve it when it protects:

- Product names, approved terminology, UI labels, or legal and technical distinctions
- SEO requirements or exact keywords, within the supplied frequency constraint
- Clarity in independently displayed strings or complex explanations
- Deliberate parallelism, contrast, emphasis, or navigation

Do not create synonym drift such as alternating among `Konto`, `Account`, `Profil`, and `Benutzerbereich` for one product concept. Vary syntax and information structure before varying protected terminology.

When adjacent labels unnecessarily repeat the same head noun, compact coordination can improve the sentence without losing the shared concept:

```text
Repetitive: Ob lokale Bereitstellung, cloudbasierte Bereitstellung oder hybride Bereitstellung: ...
Compact: Ob lokal, cloudbasiert oder hybrid: Für unterschiedliche IT-Strategien stehen passende Bereitstellungsmodelle zur Verfügung.
```

Use this only when the coordinated adjectives clearly refer to the same concept in context. Preserve the full terms when shortening would create ambiguity or erase a technical distinction.

There is no universal numeric repetition threshold. Judge density, distance, function, genre, and effect on the reader.

## Build lexical range, syntactic variety, and paragraph rhythm

A publishable article should not sound as though every sentence came from the same template. Review variety across the complete passage or article, not by forcing a different word into each sentence.

Assess three connected levels:

| Level | What to review |
|---|---|
| Lexical range | Concentrated repetition of ordinary nouns, verbs, evaluative adjectives, and transitions; precision and terminology remain more important than raw variety |
| Syntactic variety | Repeated sentence openings, subject-verb frames, clause order, enablement formulas, and sentences with nearly identical length or emphasis |
| Paragraph rhythm | Whether paragraphs alternate purposefully among framing, explanation, evidence, contrast, practical consequence, judgment, and transition rather than repeating one feature-benefit pattern |

Useful variation can come from:

- Changing which information appears first according to the paragraph's focus
- Combining closely related statements and separating an important conclusion
- Alternating direct statements with conditional, contrastive, or explanatory relationships when the meaning supports them
- Using a shorter sentence to emphasize a real conclusion after denser explanation
- Replacing a vague repeated verb with a more precise verb supported by the content
- Using an unambiguous pronoun, reference, or omission instead of repeating an ordinary noun
- Giving adjacent paragraphs different communicative functions

Do not optimize for maximum variation. Avoid:

- Cycling through synonyms for one product, feature, or technical concept
- Changing sentence length randomly or breaking connected reasoning into fragments
- Adding decorative vocabulary, metaphors, transitions, or rhetorical questions without a function
- Forcing every paragraph into the same manufactured rhythm
- Removing deliberate parallelism that clarifies a comparison or sequence

Revise in this order:

1. Mark protected terminology and necessary repetitions.
2. Identify clusters of repeated ordinary words, openings, and sentence frames.
3. Improve information order and paragraph function.
4. Vary syntax and sentence emphasis where the existing pattern remains monotonous.
5. Change vocabulary only when the alternative is equally precise and natural.
6. Read the revised passage as a whole and check that its rhythm supports the argument rather than drawing attention to itself.

Treat pervasive lexical and syntactic monotony as an article-level `Style and readability` issue. Report the pattern once with representative evidence instead of listing every repeated occurrence as a separate finding.

## Calibrate an existing CTA

Review an existing CTA according to the article's purpose and the reader's likely next step. Do not add a CTA when none was supplied unless the user explicitly asks for one.

| Reader stage or context | Typical editorial fit |
|---|---|
| Informational Fachartikel | Learn more, view a method, explore relevant functions |
| Evaluation or comparison | Check requirements, compare criteria, examine compatibility |
| Tutorial or practical guide | Try the documented step with suitable material |
| Product-led article | A clear trial or demo invitation can fit if the transition is supported |
| Approved campaign element | Stronger urgency or promotion may be intentional and should be preserved |

Urgency, exclamation marks, and strong benefit promises are not errors by themselves. Edit them when they are unsupported, misdescribe the destination, conflict with the brief, or disrupt the surrounding article. Do not weaken an accurate conversion goal merely because a softer CTA is also possible.

CTA examples are not fixed replacements:

```text
High-pressure in an informational article:
Starten Sie jetzt sofort kostenlos und revolutionieren Sie Ihren Arbeitsalltag!

Possible context-appropriate direction:
Prüfen Sie, ob die Funktionen zu Ihrem Workflow passen.
```

The second version is appropriate only when evaluation is the intended next step. Preserve the CTA's actual function, linked destination, required offer, and degree of commitment.

For a comparison guide, an evaluation-oriented CTA can invite readers to test a product against their own requirements or workflow. This is not automatically weak: it can reinforce editorial credibility and reader agency. A more conversion-focused trial or account CTA can also fit when the brief and destination support it.

## Edit by paragraph function

Before rewriting a weak passage, identify what the paragraph must accomplish.

| Paragraph function | Editorial target |
|---|---|
| Opening | Frame a concrete reader problem, decision, or relevant tension |
| Selection criteria | Establish the governing use case, distinguish the main options, and explain their practical consequences |
| Product evaluation | Connect supplied capabilities to explicit priorities, users, conditions, or trade-offs |
| Comparison conclusion | Synthesize the decision criteria instead of merely announcing that a list has ended |
| CTA transition | Offer a next step that follows from the evaluation and matches the actual destination |

These are outcome criteria, not fixed sentence patterns. Do not force every passage into a five-step template. When the original paragraph is structurally weak, however, correcting grammar while retaining its generic frame is not sufficient Redakteur-level editing.

## Handle SEO without damaging the article

Treat required keywords, spellings, and placements as constraints. Check whether repetition is necessary for search intent, terminology, or independent headings before editing it.

Flag keyword use when it creates unnatural grammar, misleading claims, or obvious stuffing. Prefer natural syntactic integration, pronouns where unambiguous, and topic development over a chain of forced exact matches. Do not silently remove a required keyword or invent a synonym that changes the search intent.

SEO does not justify:

- Repeating the same promise without new information
- Using a keyword where it changes a technical distinction
- Adding unsupported claims or comparisons
- Writing headings that do not match their sections

## Preserve address and professional register

Professional credibility does not depend on `Sie`, passive voice, or nominal style.

- Follow an explicit project or task-level address requirement.
- Preserve a consistent `du` or `Sie` system when it fits the brief.
- Prefer neutral wording when the article does not need direct address.
- Do not introduce `Sie` only because the audience is professional.
- Keep terminology accessible to the intended specialists without explaining established concepts unnecessarily.

The target is neutral-professional or formal-professional according to context, not maximum distance.

## Classify findings proportionally

Use the existing seven review dimensions. Common Fachartikel findings include:

| Observation | Primary dimension | Typical severity |
|---|---|---|
| Unsupported wording strengthens a product or performance claim | Meaning preservation | Must fix when a source or brief proves the mismatch |
| Empty or excessive promotion conflicts with the editorial format | Tone and register | Recommended |
| A supported, context-appropriate promise is merely more confident than an available alternative | No issue or Optional | KEEP |
| Repeated words or sentence frames create mechanical passage-level prose | Style and readability | Recommended |
| Several paragraphs repeat the same cadence and communicative sequence | Style and readability | Recommended; `REWRITE` can be justified when pervasive |
| A recommendation lacks a stated basis but remains understandable | Style and readability or Tone and register | Recommended when the context requires editorial judgment |
| A different natural cadence is merely possible | No issue or Optional | KEEP |
| Protected terminology repeats for clarity and consistency | No issue | KEEP |

Use `Must fix` sparingly for style. A promotional sentence is not automatically factually false. Separate unsupported claim inflation from a register mismatch.

## Choose the intervention

- Choose `KEEP` when the article is precise, coherent, context-appropriate, and any repetition serves a function.
- Choose `EDIT` when individual claims, transitions, repetitions, or an existing CTA can be corrected locally.
- Choose `REWRITE` when a template-like structure, repeated sentence architecture, unsupported promotional framing, or weak argument progression affects most of a passage and local edits would leave it mechanically assembled.

Even in a rewrite, preserve supplied facts, modality, terminology, SEO constraints, product names, quotations, examples, links, and the intended conversion step.

## Final check

Before returning a Fachartikel review, confirm that:

- The article answers a relevant question for its stated specialist audience.
- The intervention matches the requested review depth, and a justified Redakteur-level rewrite is not limited by a weak original sentence skeleton.
- Structure and paragraph progression are coherent rather than formulaic.
- Evaluations are tied to supplied functions, use cases, criteria, or evidence.
- No unsupported fact, benefit, limitation, or comparison has been added.
- Ordinary-word and sentence-pattern repetition has been addressed where it impairs reading.
- Lexical range, sentence architecture, sentence length, and paragraph rhythm support the article without disturbing terminology or deliberate parallelism.
- Product names, technical terms, and required SEO keywords remain consistent.
- Confidence, promotional intensity, and the existing CTA are supported and fit the channel and reader stage; none has been weakened without a specific reason.
- Internal or private status has not been mistaken for factual verification or wording protection.
- Address form and register are consistent without unnecessary formality.
- The final German reads like an edited Fachartikel, not an advertisement or an academic imitation.
